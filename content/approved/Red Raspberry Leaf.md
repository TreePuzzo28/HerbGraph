apothecaryApplications:
  keyChallengesAddressed: |-
    * Uterine laxity or weakness
    * Menstrual cramping, heavy flow, and cycle irregularities
    * Preparation for late-stage pregnancy and childbirth labor support
    * Mild diarrhea or gastrointestinal inflammation (via astringent tannins)
  bestPreparations: >-
    * **Nourishing Herbal Infusion:** Steep 1 ounce of dried leaf in a quart of
    boiling water for 4 to 8 hours in a sealed canning jar. This long steep is
    essential for extracting the deep mineral density of the leaf.

    * **Standard Herbal Tea:** Steep 1–2 teaspoons of dried leaf in hot water
    for 15 minutes for a quick, astringent digestive or uterine tonic.
  preparationNotes: >-
    * The taste profile of a properly dried red raspberry leaf infusion is
    remarkably similar to a rich, earthy, robust black tea, but completely
    caffeine-free, making it an excellent daily drinking base for mineral
    blends.

    * Combining raspberry leaf with a small amount of peppermint or lemon balm
    balances the dry, heavy astringency of the tannins, creating a much
    smoother, brighter palate.
  keyChemistryMechanics: >-
    * **Fragarine:** A signature alkaloid that specifically targets the smooth
    muscles of the pelvic region, assisting in regulating and toning uterine
    muscle contractions.

    * **Ellagitannins:** Plant polyphenols that exert a mild, safe astringent
    effect, drawing together and tightening relaxed or inflamed mucosal tissues.

    * **Nutrient Density:** High bio-available concentrations of calcium, iron,
    magnesium, manganese, potassium, and vitamins A, C, and E trapped within the
    leaf matrix.
  safetyContraindications: >-
    * **Pregnancy & Labor Support:** Raspberry leaf is used primarily for
    late-pregnancy and labor preparation, but the supporting evidence is largely
    traditional rather than clinical. Work with a midwife or obstetrician before
    starting regular medicinal use, and never substitute it for medical
    monitoring during pregnancy.

    * **Tannin-Induced GI Irritation:** The leaf is exceptionally tannin-rich.
    Very strong brews, or steeping beyond the recommended 4 to 8 hours, can
    cause nausea, cramping, or constipation. Shorten the steep and dilute if any
    digestive upset appears.

    * **Iron Absorption — a Notable Irony:** Despite being a premier nutritive
    source of bio-available iron, its tannins bind non-heme iron and will cut
    absorption if taken alongside meals or supplements. Drink it between meals,
    and at least an hour away from any iron supplement or iron-fortified
    formula.

    * **Blood Thinners & Pre-Surgery:** Tannin-heavy preparations may modestly
    affect platelet function. As a precaution, discontinue medicinal use at
    least two weeks before any scheduled procedure.

    * **Sensitivities & Allergies:** Reactions are uncommon, but avoid use if
    you have a known sensitivity to the rose family (Rosaceae), which includes
    strawberry, blackberry, and rosehips.

    * **Sourcing & Contamination:** Because raspberry leaf is typically bought
    in bulk, source it from a reputable supplier and inspect for mildew, mold,
    and pesticide residue—damp, downy leaves are prone to both.
