apothecaryApplications:
  keyChallengesAddressed: >-
    * **Upper Respiratory Infections:** Acute onset of colds, flu, and sinus
    congestion, especially where there is "dampness" or stuck mucus.

    * **Fungal Overgrowth:** Used topically or as a wash for tinea (athlete's
    foot) or yeast-related imbalances due to its high thymol content.

    * **Flatulent Dyspepsia:** Cold, sluggish digestion, bloating, and
    intestinal gas.

    * **Damp/Cold Stagnation:** Systemic conditions where the individual feels
    chilled, heavy, or congested.

    * **Amenorrhea:** Delayed or suppressed menstruation linked to cold
    stagnation in the pelvic bowl.
  bestPreparations: >-
    * **Warm Infusion:** A spicy, warming tea. Must be steeped while covered to
    retain the powerful volatile oils that drive its diaphoretic and
    antimicrobial actions.

    * **Steam Inhalation:** Adding fresh or dried leaves to a bowl of hot water
    and inhaling the steam is a premier method for clearing sinus infections and
    loosening stubborn lung congestion.

    * **Tincture (Fresh Plant):** A high-proof alcohol extract captures the
    acrid, spicy resins and oils, providing a portable and potent antimicrobial
    for digestive or respiratory use.
  preparationNotes: >-
    * **The "Oregano of the East":** Wild Bergamot is often used as a local,
    wild-foraged substitute for Oregano or Thyme because of its shared
    chemistry. It is much more "moving" and "heating" than Spearmint or
    Peppermint.

    * **The Honey Infusion:** Infusing the flowers and leaves into raw honey (or
    creating an oxymel with apple cider vinegar) is an excellent way to soothe a
    sore, "hot" throat while delivering antimicrobial action.
  keyChemistryMechanics: >-
    * **Thymol & Carvacrol:** Potent phenolic monoterpenes that provide
    significant broad-spectrum antimicrobial, antifungal, and antiseptic
    activity. They work by disrupting the cell membranes of pathogens.

    * **Volatile Flavonoids:** Provide antioxidant support and help relax the
    smooth muscles of the digestive and respiratory tracts.

    * **Monoterpenes:** Act as rubefacients and circulatory stimulants, moving
    blood to the surface of the skin (diaphoretic) or the site of infection.
  safetyContraindications: >-
    * **Pregnancy:** Due to its emmenagogue properties (potential to stimulate
    blood flow in the pelvic area), avoid medicinal doses during pregnancy.

    * **Topical Sensitivity:** The high thymol content can be "hot" or
    irritating to sensitive skin; always dilute in a carrier oil or use as a
    diluted wash for topical applications.
