  bestPreparations: >-
    *   **The First-Aid Styptic Powder:** This is one of yarrow's most famous
    traditional uses. Grind completely bone-dry yarrow leaves and flowers into a
    ultra-fine powder using a clean grinder. Store it in a small tin in your
    first-aid kit. Packed directly into a clean, bleeding cut, it stops blood
    flow almost instantly and disinfects the wound.

    *   **The Fever-Breaking Hot Infusion:** Steep 1 tablespoon of dried yarrow
    flowers and leaves in boiling water for 10–15 minutes **under a tight lid**.
    Drink this infusion as hot as comfortably possible at the first sign of a
    cold or fever. It acts rapidly to open the pores, relax the skin, and induce
    a deep, cleansing sweat.

    *   **Vascular Tonic Tincture:** Macerate fresh or dried flowering tops in
    80 to 100-proof alcohol for 4–6 weeks. This tincture acts as a brilliant
    systemic regulator of blood fluid dynamics, strengthening weak vein walls
    and balancing blood pressure.
  preparationNotes: >-
    > [!info] The Master Blood Regulator

    > Yarrow is a rare botanical archetype known as an amphoteric vascular
    remedy—it regulates blood flow in opposite directions depending on what the
    body requires. Applied to an open, actively bleeding wound, its intense
    astringent tannins act as a **styptic**, clotting the blood and sealing the
    tissue. Conversely, taken internally for venous stagnation, its volatile
    oils act as a **blood thinned and moving agent**, breaking up internal
    congestion, preventing blood clots, and moving fluid out of bruised tissues.
  keyChemistryMechanics: >-
    *   **The Tissue Closer (Tannins & Achilleine):** Yarrow contains the
    alkaloid **achilleine** and condensed tannins. When these compounds come
    into contact with broken blood vessels, they physically precipitate
    proteins, causing the blood to coagulate rapidly and sealing the broken
    capillaries to arrest bleeding.

    *   **The Anti-Inflammatory Blue (Chamazulene):** Just like German
    Chamomile, yarrow contains *matricin*, which converts into **chamazulene**
    during hot water extraction or distillation. This deep-blue compound blocks
    inflammatory pathways, visibly reducing swelling and heat in infected wounds
    or inflamed tissues.
  safetyContraindications: >-
    > [!warning] ⚠️ Safety & Contraindications

    > * **Pregnancy Warning:** Do **not** use internal medicinal doses of yarrow
    during pregnancy. It is a powerful **emmenagogue** that physically
    stimulates blood flow to the pelvic region and can induce uterine
    contractions. Regular external use on cuts or inside a foot bath is safe.

    > * **Asteraceae/Ragweed Allergy:** Because yarrow belongs to the Asteraceae
    family, individuals with known, severe allergies to ragweed, daisies, or
    marigolds should use it with caution, as it can occasionally trigger contact
    dermatitis or localized hives.
